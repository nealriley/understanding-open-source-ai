# Selected section: Data, Adaptation and Evaluation

Decision: [comparative research](next-section-research.md) and [two teaching trials](sequence-trials.md), reviewed 16 September 2026. The section contains three chapters. These display numbers are editorial order; IDs and URLs survive later moves.

## 2. Where model data comes from (`data`)

- **Prerequisites:** Chapters 0–1: weights/context, training, release artefacts and permissions.
- **Reader question:** What exactly is in a dataset, and why do its selection and history matter?
- **Outcomes:** distinguish data roles; explain selection and provenance; propose a split that matches a stated use and identify limitations.
- **Sequence:** state the library task → inspect one record → distinguish raw documents, demonstrations and checks → collection/filtering/mixing → documented Dolma case → provenance and permission → development/test separation → inventory task.
- **Worked material:** original six-record library inventory including obsolete and generated records, a restricted visitor message and a duplicate family. Distinguish a legitimate retrieval source from a leaked evaluation answer.
- **Output/feedback:** a row-by-row use decision, a split rule and missing coverage; compare a cautious retrieval pilot with a later adaptation plan. Explain why neither proves performance.
- **Boundaries:** no corpus-building code, legal conclusions, universal split ratios or broad safety debate. Reuse the old data chapter's questions, not its mixed risk structure.

## 3. How models are adapted (`adaptation`)

- **Prerequisites:** foundations and data roles/splits.
- **Reader question:** What supplies a training signal, and what would show a proposed change helped?
- **Outcomes:** distinguish context changes from weight updates; trace demonstrations/preferences/rewards/teacher outputs; design a controlled comparison.
- **Sequence:** diagnose a library error → choose context, retrieval or training → inspect one demonstration and one preference pair → explain reward-based training → synthetic data/teacher–student flow → primary InstructGPT/DPO cases → comparison design.
- **Worked material:** fictional notice with supported answer, a preference reversal under two rubrics, a teacher-produced unsupported claim and a four-condition study.
- **Output/feedback:** pipeline annotations and a study plan; identify confounding changes and trade-offs. No numerical uplift claims.
- **Boundaries:** mechanism names do not certify safety, truth or permission; access-abuse allegations and competitive motives remain archived. Training mathematics is deferred; probability distillation receives an optional explanation.

## 4. How to judge a model (`evaluation`)

- **Prerequisites:** data and adaptation, including a fixed baseline and protected test set.
- **Reader question:** What does a result establish about this task, under these conditions?
- **Outcomes:** write an evaluation contract and score outputs; reproduce a weighted comparison; state uncertainty and defend a conditional next step.
- **Sequence:** task/conditions → scoring rubric and disagreements → per-task counts and means → weighting reversal → small-sample and sampling uncertainty → HELM as a bounded historical case → section decision.
- **Worked material:** original answer-scoring examples plus the archived fictional Alder/Birch counts, relabelled explicitly as complete systems, not observed model results. Shared records generate the table and widget.
- **Output/feedback:** short evidence note combining dataset decisions, intervention controls and evaluation results, with two contrasting defensible recommendations and revision triggers.
- **Boundaries:** no current rankings, prices, national comparisons or formal confidence interval from invented counts. This is a section-level decision exercise, not the withdrawn final-book memo.
