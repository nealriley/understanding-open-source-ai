Study guide

This developing draft currently contains two foundations chapters. No programming experience is required. Read them in order, or use their prerequisite checks to identify what you need to revisit. The later sections and full-book decision memo will return as supporting concepts are written.

How to study

Read the explanation and worked example, attempt the application before opening its feedback, then revise your answer. Quizzes check selected distinctions; they do not replace the written work. Reading completion and quiz scores stay in your browser.

0. How Language Models Work

Explain how training changes a model and how that model generates an answer.

Make: Trace one answer through tokenisation and generation, then propose changes for new Sunday hours, brief answers and live catalogue availability. Explain where information goes, whether parameters change, required materials and a test. Add one material needed to investigate original training.

Review: Explain the generation loop and fixed weights during ordinary inference. Distinguish request context, retrieval, training and tool execution; connect each intervention to necessary materials and an appropriate test. Explain a researcher’s additional need.

1. What ‘Open’ Means

Distinguish a model release from a hosted application or model service.

Make: Assess Cedar and Rowan against the library’s adaptation and sharing requirements. Recommend a deployment route, cite the supplied terms, identify an unresolved practical question and a revision condition.

Review: Trace who operates the model; cite permissions and restrictions; distinguish absent from uninspected information; explain practical fit and a feasible next check.
