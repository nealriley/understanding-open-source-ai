Learning path

Study guide: from reading to a defensible decision

No coding is required. Keep your answers in a notebook or document. Reading times are estimates for the text; allow additional time to attempt and revise each task.

Choose a route

Full course: Chapters 0–9 in order. Engineering/product first pass: 0 → 1 → 6 → 7 → 2, then 3 → 4 → 5 → 8 → 9. Policy/journalism first pass: 0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 8, then 6 → 7 → 9. All routes complete the preparation for the final memo; optional technical disclosures can remain closed.

Read, apply, explain, revisit

Try the prerequisite check before reading. After a short section, explain its main distinction without looking. Attempt the application before opening feedback. Revise one sentence and record why. In a later session, apply the same distinction to another case. Disagreement with an author is welcome when supported by evidence and a fair counterargument.

Final case: an internal-manual assistant

Fictional case. An organisation wants an assistant for public and sensitive internal manuals. Sensitive documents must remain inside its environment. Public documents may be sent to a hosted provider. The team has limited operations capacity and has not run a task-specific evaluation. No model is known to meet its quality threshold.

Fictional options: knowns and unknowns
OptionKnownUnknown
A · hosted serviceAvailable through an API; no local weights in this offer. Can be considered for public material only.Task quality, full service terms and retention settings, price for the workload, failure handling.
B · local weightsWeights and inference software available. The fictional terms permit the intended internal use.Hardware fit, quality, operating cost, support burden and data-handling design.
C · research releaseWeights, training code, data documentation and checkpoints available. Fictional terms permit internal use.Quality, deployment requirements, support burden and whether extra research materials improve this task.

Write approximately 500–800 words. Choose a bounded next step, not an unsupported production launch. Include an evidence ledger, comparison of at least two options, an evaluation plan, strongest counterargument and a revision trigger. You may recommend delaying a component while testing another. All quantities you introduce must be labelled assumptions or proposed acceptance criteria, not measured results.

Review rubric

Score each dimension 0 (missing or contradicted), 1 (present but incomplete), or 2 (specific and justified). The maximum is 12, but this is a feedback tool, not a validated grading scale. Any violation of the sensitive-data constraint requires revision regardless of score.

Two contrasting defensible responses

These compact examples illustrate reasoning rather than complete evidence gathering. Neither reports a real experiment. Read them after drafting your own memo.

Example A: begin with a bounded local pilot of B

I recommend a limited local pilot of B, with production use deferred. Sensitive-manual access is central to the intended task, so A cannot cover the complete scope under the case constraint. B has the minimum stated artefacts and permission for a local trial. C remains a fallback if its research materials help diagnose a failure; those materials do not establish superior answers.

The strongest objection is the team’s limited operations capacity. Before involving sensitive material, an operator should test hardware fit, access controls and recovery using synthetic manuals. If the team cannot support that environment within an agreed staffing budget, stop this route. No benchmark in the book establishes B’s quality or cost.

For evaluation, define critical errors with domain reviewers, hold out representative questions and compare answers against an existing manual-search workflow. Include unanswerable questions and require source references and escalation. As a proposed pilot gate, any critical unsafe instruction blocks expansion until investigated; this is a criterion, not an observed result. Record latency, review effort, failures and total operating cost.

Revise or abandon B if the operational gate fails or held-out performance is unacceptable. My initial sentence, “Local weights are cheaper,” was unsupported. Replacing it with “Local weights satisfy the location requirement if the deployment enforces it” narrows the rationale without changing the bounded pilot recommendation.

Example B: pilot A on public manuals; defer sensitive use

I recommend a public-manual-only pilot of A while deferring the sensitive-document assistant. Limited operations capacity makes a hosted trial a plausible way to learn about the task quickly. This does not resolve the full internal-manual goal, and no sensitive text should enter the service. Confirm the service terms and retention configuration before beginning even the public-content pilot.

The strongest objection is that the pilot may teach us little about local deployment or sensitive tasks. I accept that limit. Keep its claims narrow: it can reveal question types, review effort and usability issues on public material, not prove B or C will work locally. Assign a separate owner to assess local operational feasibility; do not let the partial pilot quietly become the complete solution.

Use held-out public-manual questions, include unanswerable cases, compare with ordinary search and have reviewers identify consequential errors. Record cost per acceptable answer including review. A proposed gate is that any critical misleading answer pauses expansion for diagnosis. Evaluate exportability of the test set and results so that B and C can later be compared on suitable material under matched conditions.

Change course if service terms are unacceptable, error criteria fail or the local option becomes supportable. My initial claim, “The hosted model is more accurate,” had no case evidence. I replaced it with “Hosted access may reduce initial operational work, subject to testing.” That changes the justification and leaves accuracy explicitly unresolved.

Compare the examples on the rubric. A prioritises learning whether local deployment can meet the central constraint; B prioritises a smaller feasible trial while acknowledging deferred scope. Both must earn their evaluation and operational claims through later work. A blanket claim that all options are equivalent would avoid the decision rather than justify it.

Final review

Check every decision-relevant factual sentence. Name its source and passage or label the assumption. Confirm that no forecast became a present fact, no fictional number became a real comparison and no uninspected term became a permission. Re-score the weakest rubric dimension after revision. Learner effectiveness remains unproven until the separate pilot is conducted.
