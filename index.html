1. What 'Open' Means

Follow requests across deployment options, inspect a release package, and use its documents to assess access, permissions and practical fit.

Who this is for

Students, researchers, engineers, product teams, journalists and policy readers who want to understand open models. The main explanations and tasks require no coding. Optional technical sections and primary sources provide further depth.

Read, try, revise

Follow a fictional library assistant through generation and training, then inspect a model release’s materials and permissions. Try each chapter’s application before opening the feedback. Your reading progress is stored only in your browser.

A book in development

Later material on data, adaptation, evaluation, economics, history and governance is being researched and rewritten. The earlier chapters have been withdrawn from this reading sequence. We distinguish sourced factual claims, authors’ arguments and clearly labelled fictional practice. Dated examples are not live rankings or advice.

The project began with Nathan Lambert’s open-source AI reading list. Its essay-led debates will return where the explanations prepare readers to assess them. See the annotated sources for the current foundations.
