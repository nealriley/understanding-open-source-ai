The Lineage of Open Models

Open models did not arrive with DeepSeek. They descend from a decision OpenAI made in 2019, a volunteer Discord server in 2020, a leaked torrent in 2023, and a Seattle non-profit's insistence on publishing its data. This chapter traces the lineage in five phases and explains why the centre of gravity moved from San Francisco and Menlo Park to Hangzhou and Beijing. The interactive timeline has every date.

What you will be able to do

  • Name the key releases in each phase and explain what each changed.
  • Explain what Pythia and OLMo contribute that Llama does not.
  • Describe the DeepSeek V3/R1 moment and why it was an efficiency shock before it was a capability shock.
  • Summarise the 2026 Chinese frontier (GLM-5.x, Kimi K3, DeepSeek V4, Qwen 3.x) and Meta's retreat.

Phase 1 (2019–2022): staged releases and open science

The modern open-weights debate starts in February 2019, when OpenAI announced GPT-2 and declined to release the full 1.5B-parameter model, citing potential misuse. It released progressively larger versions over nine months. This was the first "staged release", and it created the template for every argument since: critics called it a publicity stunt, supporters called it responsible, and the model turned out to be neither as dangerous nor as useful as either side claimed. GPT-3 (May 2020) settled OpenAI's position: 175B parameters, API only, never released.

The response came from outside the labs. EleutherAI, a group that formed on Discord in 2020, released GPT-Neo, GPT-J 6B (2021) and GPT-NeoX-20B (2022) as open replications of GPT-3-class models, trained on The Pile, a dataset they also published. BigScience, a thousand-researcher collaboration coordinated by Hugging Face, trained BLOOM 176B (July 2022) on a French public supercomputer. And in April 2023 EleutherAI published Pythia, "a suite of 16 LLMs all trained on public data seen in the exact same order and ranging in size from 70M to 12B parameters", with 154 checkpoints each. Pythia was never a competitive assistant. It was, and remains, the reference tool for studying memorisation, the effect of data frequency, and how capabilities emerge during training, because it is the only thing you can run a controlled experiment on.

Key ideaTwo lineages, two goals. The "open science" lineage (EleutherAI → BigScience → Pythia → OLMo) optimises for reproducibility. The "open weights" lineage (LLaMA → Llama 2 → Qwen → DeepSeek → Kimi/GLM) optimises for capability and adoption. They overlap in tooling and people, but they answer different questions, and conflating them is the source of most confusion about what "open" delivers.

Phase 2 (2023–2024): Llama and the Cambrian explosion

Meta released the original LLaMA (7B–65B) in February 2023 to approved researchers only. Within a week the weights were on BitTorrent. Within a month Stanford's Alpaca had fine-tuned the 7B model on 52,000 instructions generated by OpenAI's text-davinci-003 for about $600, producing a passable chatbot and demonstrating, in public, how cheap distillation had become. Vicuna, Koala and a hundred others followed.

Meta made the strategy explicit with Llama 2 (July 2023, commercial license) and Llama 3 (April 2024), and then with Llama 3.1 405B (July 2024), the first open-weights model at frontier scale, accompanied by Zuckerberg's letter (Chapter 2). In parallel, Mistral 7B (September 2023) and Mixtral 8x7B (December 2023) showed a European startup could set the small-model frontier, and Alibaba's Qwen line, beginning in 2023 and hitting its stride with Qwen 2.5 (September 2024), quietly became the most fine-tuned family on Hugging Face.

On the science side, Ai2 released OLMo in February 2024 with weights, the Dolma pretraining corpus, training code and evaluation code, explicitly to counter the fact that "the most powerful models have become closed off". OLMo 2 (November 2024; 7B, 13B and later 32B) added a stability-focused architecture, the "Dolmino" midtraining mix, the Tülu 3 post-training recipe, and RLVR, placing its base models "at the Pareto frontier of performance to training compute" against Llama 3.1 and Qwen 2.5. This was the period Lambert calls the "Cambrian explosion": dozens of credible open models, most of which nobody adopted, because adoption concentrates on a few winners.

Phase 3 (late 2024–2025): DeepSeek and the Chinese takeover

Two releases from a Hangzhou hedge-fund spinout reset the field. DeepSeek V3 (December 26, 2024) was a 671B-parameter MoE with 37B active, trained, according to its paper, for a final run costing about $5.6M in GPU time, using a set of efficiency techniques (multi-head latent attention, auxiliary-loss-free load balancing, FP8 training) that Western labs had not combined at scale. DeepSeek R1 (January 20, 2025) added a published RLVR recipe and matched OpenAI's o1 with visible chains of thought, under an MIT license.

The market reaction was the largest single-day loss of value in stock-market history (Nvidia fell roughly $600B on January 27). The product reaction was faster: Perplexity added R1 to its search product within eight days, hosted on US servers, and Kevin Xu's "Structural Advantage" essay records that Alibaba shipped Qwen 2.5 Max eight days after R1 to avoid being left behind. The ATOM Report later dated the moment "Chinese models overtook their counterparts built in the U.S." in adoption to the summer of 2025, when Kimi K2 (Moonshot, July 2025, ~1T parameters) and GLM-4.5 (Z.ai, July 2025) landed as the first open models built for agentic coding.

The US response was partial. Meta's Llama 4 (April 2025) landed badly and its Behemoth model was shelved. OpenAI released GPT-OSS 120B and 20B (August 2025) under Apache 2.0, its first open weights since GPT-2, but the models did not dent Chinese adoption. Nvidia's Nemotron line, distilled partly from Chinese open models, became the main US-origin open family in production use. Ai2's Olmo 3 (November 2025; 7B and 32B) shipped the "entire model flow" and the "strongest fully-open thinking model released to-date", but at 32B it was two orders of magnitude smaller than the Chinese frontier.

Source noteDeepSeek's "$5.6M" figure refers to the final pretraining run's GPU rental cost as stated in the V3 paper; it excludes research, prior runs, data and hardware ownership. It was widely misreported as the total cost of building DeepSeek. Chapter 7 covers the parallel claim, also widely circulated, that R1 was distilled from o1, and why Lambert argues it was not.

His reading of K3 is the important one for this book: it "represents a watershed moment signaling China's commitment to open-source AI development", with the gap compressed "from 6–9 months to approximately 3–5 months".

How Z.ai keeps pace

The GLM-5.3 essay is the best window into the mechanism. Zhipu, founded in 2019 out of Tsinghua's THUDM group, published GLM in March 2021, GLM-130B in August 2022, and ChatGLM within four months of ChatGPT. It kept its 2024 flagship closed, watched DeepSeek's success, and reversed: "You need to expand the cake first and then take a bite," as its product director told ChinaTalk. By July 2026 it had $1B in annual recurring revenue. Lambert credits its parity not to distillation but to post-training execution, release velocity (Chinese labs ship within days of training; US labs take months of safety testing, during which they keep optimising), and aggressive benchmark focus driven by being a public company. GLM-5.3 at ~750B parameters matching models several times larger is, he argues, evidence that "reproducing RL environments and training infrastructure cannot be simply extracted from frontier models".

Phase 5: Meta's retreat and the missing Western champion

The lineage has a hole in it. Meta, which defined the open-weights era from 2023 to 2025, shelved Llama 4 Behemoth, released the closed Muse Spark in April 2026, and had its CEO state that superintelligence would not be released openly. Gurley reads this as a strategic reversal; Lambert reads it as confirmation that open models "lack the central economic champion" that would represent their interests in Washington. Mistral (675B flagship, $400M ARR, $830M raised) is the closest Western open contender; Google's Gemma and OpenAI's GPT-OSS are partial; Nvidia's Nemotron is a derivative family; Ai2 and Thinking Machines are the principled but small releasers. This vacuum is the setting for the policy fight in Chapter 8.

The pattern

  • Every phase was started by an efficiency or recipe leak, not a capability lead. Alpaca (cheap distillation), Mistral 7B (small-model quality), DeepSeek V3 (training cost), R1 (published RL recipe).
  • Adoption concentrates. Hundreds of models are released; a handful (Llama, then Qwen, then DeepSeek/Kimi/GLM) capture most derivatives and inference volume.
  • Fully open stays small. Pythia topped out at 12B, OLMo 2 at 32B, Olmo 3 at 32B. The science lineage has never matched the scale of the adoption lineage.
  • The open frontier moves to whoever has both the motive and the compute. In 2023 that was Meta. In 2026 it is a set of Chinese companies with government backing, GPU scarcity that rewards efficiency, and a domestic culture Chapter 5 describes.
